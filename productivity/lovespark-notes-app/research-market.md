## Demand Tiers: What Users Actually Want

### TIER 1 — Massive Demand, Clear Signal

**1. Reading Enhancement (ADHD-specific)**
- Bionic reading (bold word beginnings), text chunking, pacing, TTS
- Most ADHD-specific extensions exist here
- Leaders: Helperbird (1M+), Speechify (50M+), Nook, ADHD Reader
- Gap: fragmented — need 3-4 extensions to cover what should be one tool

**2. Gentle Anti-Doomscroll (NOT hard blocking)**
- Community consensus: hard blockers fail because users bypass them and feel shame
- Gentle approaches winning: ImpulsePause (5-8s pause), Stop Doomscroll (breathing exercises)
- Users want friction + awareness, not walls
- Gap: nothing ADHD-specific, mostly generic screen time tools

**3. All-In-One ADHD Browser Companion**
- The #1 meta-complaint: each need = separate extension, managing them = executive function burden
- Users want: reading mode + tab management + doomscroll prevention + break reminders + sensory reduction in ONE package
- Gap: **nobody has built this**

**4. Free, Privacy-First, No Account Required**
- The ADHD community is loud about not wanting paywalls on accessibility tools
- ADHD disproportionately affects financial stability
- Most praised tools are free, local-only, open source
- Gap: most popular tools are SaaS with subscriptions

### TIER 2 — Strong Demand, Growing Fast

**5. Time Blindness Tools**
- Core ADHD symptom, no good browser solution
- Users want time-awareness embedded in browser, not a separate app
- Physical Time Timer is beloved — no digital browser equivalent
- Gap: **completely unaddressed in browser extensions**

**6. Tab Overwhelm / Smart Tab Management**
- 50-200+ tabs as "external working memory" — closing feels like losing thoughts
- OneTab too passive, ADHD Tab Manager (5 tabs) too restrictive
- Skipper auto-saves unused tabs — closest to what users want
- Gap: gentle archival without feeling like things are lost

**7. Sensory-Friendly Web Mode**
- Flashing ads, autoplay, cluttered layouts cause genuine distress
- Dark Reader handles darkness but not calm; uBlock handles ads but not visual overwhelm
- Users want: muted palettes globally, animation blocking, simplified navigation, decluttered sidebars
- Gap: no holistic "calm the entire web" tool

**8. Hyperfocus Break Reminders**
- Users forget to eat, drink, take meds during hyperfocus
- Phone alarms get dismissed
- Want: browser-native, persistent but non-intrusive, adaptive not nagging
- Gap: **no browser-native solution exists**

### TIER 3 — Emerging Demand, Blue Ocean

**9. Emotional Regulation / RSD Support**
- Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria affects up to 99% of ADHD adults
- Users triggered by comment sections, social media interactions, email
- Want: content warnings, grounding exercises, "step back" overlays
- Gap: **zero browser tools address this**

**10. Task Initiation / Paralysis Support**
- One of the most debilitating ADHD symptoms
- NeuroFlow (website-contextual todos) is only attempt, very new
- Want: micro-steps, lowered barriers, contextual nudges
- Gap: enormous

**11. Context-Switching / Transition Support**
- ADHD users struggle switching between tasks
- Want: "transition ritual" tools that save/restore mental context
- Gap: **completely unaddressed**

**12. Body Doubling (Browser-Based)**
- Virtual body doubling proven 3x more likely to finish tasks
- Focusmate, Flow Club are standalone apps — nothing in-browser
- Growing 45% YoY
- Gap: no browser extension version

**13. Gamified Self-Care**
- Finch ($2M/month!) proves the model
- Gentle, non-punitive, dopamine-friendly
- Gap: no browser extension gamified system

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Bundle power:** Your 41 extensions ARE the all-in-one suite — you just need a hub to unify them. This is your moat.

2. **"Free, privacy-first, neurodivergent-made"** is a positioning no competitor occupies at scale. Every major player (Helperbird, Speechify, Tiimo, Inflow) charges subscriptions.

3. **iOS priority for revenue:** The money is in iOS apps. Tiimo ($200K/month), Finch ($2M/month), Forest ($140K/month). Your med-tracker is in the right space — expand from there.

4. **The gentle approach wins:** Community is clear — punitive tools fail ADHD users. LoveSpark's pink, friendly, Sparky-mascot brand is perfectly positioned for the gentle/encouraging approach.

5. **Gamification = retention:** ADHD users abandon tools within days. Gamification (Forest trees, Finch pet, Habitica quests) is the proven retention mechanism. Sparky could evolve into a progression system.
